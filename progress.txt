### Certificate: slack measured, DFPN retired (2026-08-10)

Question: the certifier proves wins only in a restricted subgame -- S may step
strictly toward goal, plus one equal-distance re-commit after an opponent wall.
Any win needing a sideways step is unprovable by construction (the motivating
case is arena game 1922, where the winning moves were b3 and a4-sideways).
certify_dfpn on feat/dfpn-certificate exists to lift that restriction with a
full AND/OR proof search, 433 lines plus GHI-safety machinery.

Measured the cheap version instead: a slack budget of N non-improving steps,
sound for free because restricting the maximiser can never create a false
positive. Equal-distance-only slack (switching between equally short routes)
beat any-non-improving slack at identical rescues and up to 65% fewer nodes --
a backward step is wandering, not another shortest path.

Result over 1800 random low-wall positions:

  walls   slack0 proves   slack1 rescues   nodes/pos 0 -> 1
   <= 2    579/600 96.5%    +2  (9.5%)        126 -> 231
   <= 4    550/600 91.7%    +0  (0.0%)        411 -> 422
   <= 8    549/600 91.5%    +2  (3.9%)        169 -> 218

  slack 2 and 3 rescue nothing beyond slack 1, at any budget.

So: +4 positions in 1800 (0.2%) for 3-83% more certify nodes. Left at 0.

The finding that matters is negative: the toward-goal restriction is NOT what
limits the certifier. It already proves 91-96%; the failures are positions
where the win is not forced, or the node budget ran out -- not positions where
a sideways step was forbidden. DFPN's entire premise is lifting that
restriction, so it would be solving the wrong problem. Retired, not merged.

Old DFPN gates cannot be used as evidence either way: 52W-71L over 123 games is
p = 0.087 (interval -120..+8, contains zero), the per-move telemetry is
statistically identical to main (same think time, depth, stop-reason mix), so
the feature was almost certainly inert, and the runs used 541 human openings
rather than the 39 arena lines. The branch is also pre-purge and reintroduces
legacy_search; it does not build.

## Walls-in-hand embedding: 121, direct-indexed

index = own_walls * 11 + opp_walls. No remap, no lookup, multiply-and-add.
Configs that never appear are never trained and stay zero, which is exactly
neutral. Not compacted: a remap would save 768 bytes and could hand the ~1% of
(0,1) positions that DO reach the net another configuration's vector.

Measured over all 1,405,888 stored positions:
  - 109 of 121 configs appear at all; 106 after excluding cert-solved
  - samples per config: min 1, p10 39, median 2,384, max 66,538
  - 29 configs have <500 samples -- thinnest are (8,0)=1, (9,1)=1, (10,3)=2
  - CERT-SOLVED CONFIGS HOLD 456,611 POSITIONS = 32% OF THE STORE
    (0,0)=305,073  (0,1)=80,738  (1,0)=70,800
    The certificate answers these before the net is consulted, so a third of
    every epoch was being spent on positions the evaluator never sees.

Train with a minimum-sample threshold: below it, no gradient. A vector learned
from one sample is worse than no vector. Thresholds resolve themselves as
self-play fills configs in -- no format change, no retrain of anything else.

## Targets: blend, do not choose

Three signals, opposite trade-offs:
  - game outcome   1 bit/game, sparse and noisy, but GROUND TRUTH
  - teacher eval   dense and low-variance, but another engine's OPINION,
                   calibrated to ITS search and architecture
  - proven/cert    exact, but only near the end

The 2.28M zero.ink labels are `teacher_value` -- i.e. all of the second kind.
Distilling them alone teaches that engine's blind spots and a value scale
tuned to its search. Target = lambda*teacher + (1-lambda)*outcome, lambda
SWEPT not assumed. The outcome term anchors the net to what actually wins.

Their games also supply POSITIONS our engine never generates. That value is
independent of whether their eval transfers.

## Curriculum: outward from the solved frontier

1. Generate endgame positions that are NOT instantly certificate-solvable,
   search them, and take the certificate's verdict as an EXACT label. This is
   tablebase-quality ground truth, not an estimate.
2. Walk outward from the frontier until proving a position stops being
   practical. That boundary ends the endgame phase.
3. Then blended teacher+outcome data for the middlegame and opening.

Data sufficiency is NOT a constraint: self-play generates as much as needed.

## Search dump, not TT dump

Training on searched scores is right, but the TT CANNOT be dumped for this:
SharedTtEntry stores key_hi/key_lo only -- hashes, not positions, and a 64-bit
hash is not invertible. Instrument the STORE PATH instead to emit
(packed_state, score, depth, bound) as entries are written during self-play.

Only EXACT-bound entries are valid targets. Cutoff entries mean ">= this" and
fail-lows mean "<= this"; they are the MAJORITY of the table, and training on
them as values teaches numbers that were never established.

## Endgame phase, restated -- this is the important one

The point is NOT to train on positions the certificate answers instantly;
those never reach the net and are excluded ((0,0),(0,1),(1,0), 32% of the
store). The point is the FRONTIER: positions the certificate CAN prove given
time, but the engine CANNOT prove in ~1s at the board. Those do reach the net,
and today it guesses at them.

The asymmetry is the whole method: spend unlimited OFFLINE compute to produce
labels the engine can never afford at play time. A 60s proof search offline
becomes free knowledge inside a 1s game search. The net stops re-deriving what
is already known and starts from the truth.

It compounds. Each proven shell extends the frontier: positions 20 moves out
become evaluable without proof, so the search reaches further within the same
clock, which makes the next shell provable. Everything later in the curriculum
inherits this, which is why it must come first.

Phase ends where a long offline search can no longer prove the position.

VALIDATION WITHOUT A GATE: hold out proven positions, ask the net for a STATIC
eval (no search), and measure sign agreement against the proven verdict. Costs
seconds, isolates exactly what this phase is supposed to buy, and tells us
whether it worked before spending 4 hours of games on it. A game gate cannot
isolate this; that metric can.

## Phase 1 generation: retrograde first, forward proving second

Better than generating positions forward and hoping they prove: work BACKWARD
from solved states. Every position produced is then solvable by construction,
no search is wasted on positions that never prove, and distance-to-win falls
out of the expansion depth instead of needing a search at all.

Base already exists: `tbgen` solves 12,960 states (7,119 W / 5,841 L, 0 draws,
hash e9f5e9817371bcef) in 3ms -- 81*80*2, the complete no-walls-on-board race.

COMPLETE retrograde dies fast. Wall placements multiply the state space:
~1.7M with one wall on board, ~105M with two, ~4.4B with three.

So SAMPLE the retrograde frontier instead of enumerating it. Pick a solved
position, generate predecessors, keep a sample, repeat. Every sample still
carries an exact label; sampling rate controls cost instead of combinatorics
controlling it.

Exploit the asymmetry: backward WIN propagation is cheap -- one move into a
position lost for the opponent proves the predecessor, so a single witness
suffices. Backward LOSS propagation needs every successor checked. Harvest
exactly-labelled wins cheaply first; take losses from the complete shells near
the base where enumeration is still affordable.

Retrograde and forward proving COMPOSE rather than compete: retrograde covers
the base exactly, and forward search covers the band beyond where retrograde
stops. `score-out` is the bridge -- it already reports `proven`, `bound`,
`score`, `depth` per position, e.g.
  titanium score-out --moves e2 e8 e3 --nodes 200000
  {"schema":"score-out-v1","score":48,"bound":"exact","proven":false,...}

MISSING ONE FIELD: score-out does not echo the position, so a verdict cannot be
keyed to a board. Add `packed` (hex of pack_state(&game)) to its JSON before
using it as a label generator -- pack_state must be called BEFORE `game` is
moved into TitaniumSearch::production. This edit was started and rejected; the
tree is clean.

Per user: include even instantly-solvable positions in phase 1. Ground truth on
the final buckets is worth more than the wasted capacity, because everything
later in the curriculum inherits it.

## Build the tablebase as a GRAPH, not a dense array — decided before compute

Walls are never returned, so the state space is a LAYERED DAG. Cycles exist
only WITHIN a layer (pawns shuffling on a fixed wall configuration); every wall
placement is a strictly one-way edge into a lower layer. Consequences:

- Solve BOTTOM-UP by walls in hand. When a layer is solved, every
  wall-placement edge out of the layer above points at an already-solved
  entry, so those successors are lookups, not recursion.
- Within a layer only pawn moves remain. That is the cyclic part and needs
  proper retrograde with repetition handling — exactly what the existing
  12,960-state race solver already does. Reuse it per wall configuration.

WHY A GRAPH, precisely. Two things get conflated here and only one is a real
win:

- Transposition merging is NOT the reason. Key on the wall BITBOARD rather
  than the move order and different move sequences reaching the same walls
  land on the same entry automatically. A dense array indexed by wall-set
  gets that for free.
- SPARSITY is the reason. C(128,k) is astronomically large while the
  configurations that ever arise are a vanishing fraction of it. Dense
  allocation is impossible past a couple of walls. Store
  `(wall_bits, pawn0, pawn1, stm) -> (result, distance)` in a hash and only
  reached states cost anything.

STREAM IT. This tablebase is scaffolding, not a shipped artifact: solve one
layer, emit its labelled positions as training samples, DISCARD the solver
state, move to the next. Nothing needs to stay resident, so memory stops being
the limit and only generation compute remains one. This is what makes it
survivable on a laptop with limited RAM.

WHAT SHIPS: only the net, plus the zero-wall tablebase the engine already
computes at runtime in 3ms with no file to load. The large tablebase exists to
be distilled into the NNUE's low-wall buckets and then deleted. The net is a
lossy compression of it.

Cost ladder is set by walls IN HAND, not walls on board (an earlier note in
this file sized the wrong axis). With (0,0) in hand there are no wall moves at
all, so the position is a pure race on fixed scenery: ~12,960 states per wall
configuration, milliseconds each, exactly labelled. Each additional wall in
hand multiplies by ~128 placements, so (1,0) is ~128x a race solve and (1,1)
~128^2. That is where it stops, and it matches going 1 wall, then 2, then 3,
and halting when it no longer fits.

## Correction to the layer-by-layer plan: TB(0,0) is not one object

Zero walls IN HAND means twenty walls ON BOARD, so TB(0,0) is a FAMILY indexed
by which twenty walls are placed -- C(128,20)-ish members. "Solve 0/0
completely, then work outward to 1/0 and 0/1" is not executable: the base case
is not a single tablebase.

It fails from the other end too. Solving a position with k walls placed needs
its successors, which have k+1 placed, so exhaustive construction is infeasible
in both directions. Wall irreversibility is real and useful but does not by
itself make the space enumerable.

WHAT IS EXECUTABLE: lazy, memoised solving over REACHABLE positions. Do not
construct layers. Take a position, recurse into successors, memoise on
(wall_bits, pawn0, pawn1, stm), and rely on irreversibility to bound the
wall-placement recursion. Only configurations that actually arise cost
anything, and each solved one becomes a reusable oracle for its predecessors.

So the starting point is NOT a new tablebase builder: the certificate and race
solver already do on-demand proof with memoisation. Instrument them to emit
(packed_state, result, distance) as they run and drive them over sampled

THREE-VALUED, NOT TWO. The existing 12,960-state tablebase reports 0 draws
because a pure race cannot repeat -- someone always advances. With walls in
hand, pawns can shuffle and repetition draws are real. A win/loss-only solver
would silently mislabel drawn cycles as losses, poisoning exactly the ground
truth this phase exists to produce.
