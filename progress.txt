### Certificate: slack measured, DFPN retired (2026-08-10)

Question: the certifier proves wins only in a restricted subgame -- S may step
strictly toward goal, plus one equal-distance re-commit after an opponent wall.
Any win needing a sideways step is unprovable by construction (the motivating
case is arena game 1922, where the winning moves were b3 and a4-sideways).
certify_dfpn on feat/dfpn-certificate exists to lift that restriction with a
full AND/OR proof search, 433 lines plus GHI-safety machinery.

Measured the cheap version instead: a slack budget of N non-improving steps,
sound for free because restricting the maximiser can never create a false
positive. Equal-distance-only slack (switching between equally short routes)
beat any-non-improving slack at identical rescues and up to 65% fewer nodes --
a backward step is wandering, not another shortest path.

Result over 1800 random low-wall positions:

  walls   slack0 proves   slack1 rescues   nodes/pos 0 -> 1
   <= 2    579/600 96.5%    +2  (9.5%)        126 -> 231
   <= 4    550/600 91.7%    +0  (0.0%)        411 -> 422
   <= 8    549/600 91.5%    +2  (3.9%)        169 -> 218

  slack 2 and 3 rescue nothing beyond slack 1, at any budget.

So: +4 positions in 1800 (0.2%) for 3-83% more certify nodes. Left at 0.

The finding that matters is negative: the toward-goal restriction is NOT what
limits the certifier. It already proves 91-96%; the failures are positions
where the win is not forced, or the node budget ran out -- not positions where
a sideways step was forbidden. DFPN's entire premise is lifting that
restriction, so it would be solving the wrong problem. Retired, not merged.

Old DFPN gates cannot be used as evidence either way: 52W-71L over 123 games is
p = 0.087 (interval -120..+8, contains zero), the per-move telemetry is
statistically identical to main (same think time, depth, stop-reason mix), so
the feature was almost certainly inert, and the runs used 541 human openings
rather than the 39 arena lines. The branch is also pre-purge and reintroduces
legacy_search; it does not build.

## Measurements worth not repeating

- Engine speed is ~833k nps, single thread, production config. NOT 994k (that
  was the bench running a non-production config) and NOT 491k (that was the
  instrumented build). --features bench-instrument costs roughly half the speed
  and distorts per-phase percentages unevenly, so use it for the ORDER of
  phases only, never to answer "how fast is the engine".
- Where time goes (instrumented, so magnitudes are inflated but the ranking is
  usable): evaluate 36%, refresh_dist 21%, distance LRU ~8%, eval_nnue_prep 8%
  (more than double eval_nnue_infer at 3.5%), wall_legality 2%, gen_moves 2.4%.
  eval_cat_heat reads 0.00% and CAT builds fire on 2.1% of nodes, so the
  "CAT costs 47% of search time" claim in TRAINING_HANDOFF.md is STALE.
- The engine uses 57.7% of the time it is allocated. Only 20% of moves reach
  time_up; 35% quit on easy_move_stable after ~46% of budget; 14% on
  forced_mate_or_loss after 8.5%.
- Perfect move ordering is worth about +0.8 ply, measured by degrading ordering
  and reading the gradient (~1 ply per 12-13 points of accuracy). Alpha-beta
  still has to refute every alternative; ordering only decides how cheaply.
  Caveat: that experiment perturbed only the FIRST move, so it does not measure
  damage from systematically misranking the tail. See open item 1.
- Certificate proof rate by walls in hand (corpus): (0,0) 1.00, (0,1) 0.99,
  (0,2) 0.84, (1,1) 0.73, (1,2) 0.31, (2,2) 0.11. The gap worth attacking is
  (1,1) and (1,2), where NEITHER side is broke, so the broke-side theorems and
  the race solver both sit out.
- Zero-wall tablebase is intact and merged: tbgen solves 12,960 states
  (7,119 W / 5,841 L / 0 draws, hash e9f5e9817371bcef) in 3ms, computed at
  runtime with no file to load.

## Open work, highest value first

1. ORDERING IS NOT SATURATED — the "84.6% best-move-first" figure came from an
   old memory note and was never re-verified this session. Reading
   order_moves_prior:

     tt_move           2_000_000_000
     pawn move         ~1_000_000 - dist*1000      (995k-1M)
     killer[0]           900_000
     countermove         870_000
     killer[1]           850_000
     everything else     history, else CAT heat, often 0

   EVERY pawn move outranks EVERY killer. A killer is evidence (it just caused a
   cutoff in a sibling node); pawn progress is a guess, and the guess wins. And
   a wall move with no history yet and low CAT heat scores 0, so it lands around
   rank 100 of ~130 and takes the deepest LMR reduction. That is a plausible
   blindness mechanism and it is NOT what the saturation claim measured.

   WIP is stashed on branch diag/ordering-quality (see git stash list): a
   cutoff-rank histogram (bench_instr::bump_cut_rank plus cut_rank_hist /
   cut_rank_pawn / cut_rank_wall) answering "where does the refutation actually
   sit in our ordering", split by move type. The instrumentation compiles and
   runs. Only the report block in search_bench.rs needs relocating: it was
   inserted inside an enclosing with_bench and panicked with "RefCell already
   borrowed". Fix: copy the three arrays into locals inside ONE with_bench,
   print outside it, and place the whole block BEFORE take_json_report().

   If cutoffs commonly sit at rank 20+, the fix is cheap and real: put killers
   above pawn moves, and give unproven wall moves a floor so they do not sort to
   score 0 where LMR cuts hardest.

4. Policy planes 9/10 (own/opponent shortest-path mask) are HARDCODED ZERO at

   gradient. Plausible contributor to the flywheel plateau at 65% top-1.
   Design if resumed: feed 1-3 witness paths per player (PathWitness and

   for movegen, so the planes are nearly free), randomise the count during
   training, and add an extra path or two in the opening. Quoridor is about
   forcing a choice between routes, so a single-path encoding hides the point.

5. Do NOT replace CAT with a policy head for ORDERING: there is no speed to
   reclaim (2.1% of nodes, 0.00% eval timer) and ordering accuracy is capped at
   ~0.8 ply. If policy is wired anywhere, wire it to modulate REDUCTION depth,
   which is continuous and has real headroom. Apply it WITHIN a tier, never
   across: ordering spans +/-2e9 in hard tiers, so a raw logit is invisible
   until the weight is large enough to override the TT move, at which point it
   is harmful.

## Process rules learned the expensive way

- One branch, one behavioural commit, branched fresh from main, diff-verified
  before building. A 12-commit candidate produced an uninterpretable gate.
- No opt-in strength switches. The branch must play at full strength with no
  environment set. Delete the old path rather than disabling it.
- This harness shows +/-30-40 Elo between IDENTICAL binaries at n=60-100
  (gate_control_AvA 0.441, gate_control_AvA_clean 0.489). Nothing under roughly
  50 Elo is decidable at 100 games. Ten gates ran today; one hit p=0.0088 and
  did not replicate. That is what multiple comparisons looks like.
- Gates need at least 2 threads per engine. At 1 thread Lazy SMP and the shared
  TT are dead code, which is how the frozen-1M-slot TT survived unmeasured.
- With pondering off only one side searches at a time, so 8 workers x 2 threads
  is still only 8 active threads. Running 4 workers left half the machine idle;
  verify 8 works before the next long gate.
